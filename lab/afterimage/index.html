Afterimage

Upload a photo and a small neural network — trained once, ahead of time, on thousands of other photos — rebuilds it from a 256-number impression. No copy of your photo ever leaves your browser, and the network itself never sees it either: it only ever sees the impression.

How this actually works — the two-phase architecture, explained

There are two completely separate phases here, and only one of them ever touches your photo.

Training happened once, ahead of time, on a GPU, using 13,000 ordinary photos of animals, vehicles, and people — never yours. A pair of networks, an encoder and a decoder, learned together to compress a photo down to 256 numbers and rebuild it from just those numbers.

Rendering is what happens in your browser just now. Only the decoder came along for the ride — the encoder was left behind entirely, because it turns out a decoder on its own, searching for the best 256 numbers for a specific new photo, reconstructs better than the encoder's one-shot guess ever could. That search is the progress bar you just watched.

The gray blocks are actual photo pixels. Coral is the encoder, which only ever existed on the training machine. Teal is the decoder — the exact same one, unchanged, that just ran in your browser. Violet is the 256-number latent vector: the entire "impression" your photo gets reduced to before the decoder rebuilds it.
