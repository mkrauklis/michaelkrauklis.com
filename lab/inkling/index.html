03 — look inside

Look inside the network

The whole network is only three layers: your drawing becomes 100 pixel values, every one of which connects to all 18 hidden neurons below — the network's one and only hidden layer — which in turn connect to 26 output neurons, one per letter A–Z. The 18 tiles below are those 18 hidden neurons: each has learned to look for one pattern of ink across your drawing grid — its receptive field. The larger grid to the right shows how strongly those same 18 neurons vote for each of the 26 letters. Both update live as you train.

Each tile is one hidden neuron's real weights, not a stylized icon: its border color is that neuron's bias, and its interior is its 64 input weights — one per pixel — reshaped back into the 8×8 drawing grid, rose where a pixel excites that neuron and blue where it inhibits it. The heatmap is the next layer's weights: one column per output letter, one row per hidden neuron (the same 18, same order as the tiles), again rose/blue for how much that neuron pushes toward or away from that letter.

How this actually works — the whole network, in one screen

Every drawing is downsampled to an 8×8 grid — 64 numbers between 0 (blank) and 1 (full ink). That's the entire input. From there it's the plainest network there is:

Forward: a₁ = tanh(W₁x + b₁), then ŷ = softmax(W₂a₁ + b₂). Loss: cross-entropy against the label you gave it. Backward: the standard chain rule — δ₂ = ŷ − y, then δ₁ = (W₂ᵀδ₂) ⊙ (1 − a₁²) — with every W and b nudged by −η·∂L. Pressing Train adds your sample to everything taught so far and re-runs several passes over all of it, so earlier letters don't get forgotten as new ones arrive.

Total parameters: 100×18+18 hidden, 18×26+26 output = 2,312 numbers. At 8 bits each that's 2,312 bytes — genuinely too dense to fit in one QR code at this network's size, confirmed directly against the real QR library rather than assumed. At 4 bits it's 1,156 bytes, which fits comfortably, so the QR export in step 04 always quantizes to 4 bits — there's no real choice to offer there. Loading weights back in only restores the numbers, not this session's drawn examples — teaching is one-way unless you keep the browser tab open.
