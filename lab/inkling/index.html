How this actually works — the whole network, in one screen

Every drawing is downsampled to a 10×10 grid — 100 numbers between 0 (blank) and 1 (full ink). That's the entire input. From there it's the plainest network there is:

Forward: a₁ = tanh(W₁x + b₁), then ŷ = softmax(W₂a₁ + b₂). Loss: cross-entropy against the label you gave it. Backward: the standard chain rule — δ₂ = ŷ − y, then δ₁ = (W₂ᵀδ₂) ⊙ (1 − a₁²) — with every W and b nudged by −η·∂L. Pressing Train adds your sample to everything taught so far and re-runs several passes over all of it, so earlier letters don't get forgotten as new ones arrive.

Total parameters: 100×18+18 hidden, 18×26+26 output = 2,312 numbers. At 8 bits each that's 2,312 bytes; at 4 bits, 1,156 — small enough to ride along in a QR code, which is what step 04 is for. Loading weights back in only restores the numbers, not this session's drawn examples — teaching is one-way unless you keep the browser tab open.
